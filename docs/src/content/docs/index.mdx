<pre class="hero-prompt"><span class="prompt-char">il[1]&gt;</span> ldc.i4 6
<span class="prompt-stack">  ┊ [int32]</span>
<span class="prompt-char">il[1]&gt;</span> ldc.i4 7
<span class="prompt-stack">  ┊ [int32, int32] ◂ top</span>
<span class="prompt-char">il[1]&gt;</span> mul
<span class="prompt-stack">  ┊ [int32]</span>
<span class="prompt-char">il[1]&gt;</span> ret
<span class="prompt-result">  = 42 : int32</span></pre>

<div class="feature-strip">
  <div class="feature-item">
    <span class="feature-key">stack</span>
    <span class="feature-label">Typed, and shown after every instruction. Underflows and type mismatches are caught at the prompt.</span>
  </div>
  <div class="feature-item">
    <span class="feature-key">ret</span>
    <span class="feature-label">Ends the cell. It compiles to a real method, runs, and prints the result with its type.</span>
  </div>
  <div class="feature-item">
    <span class="feature-key">opcodes</span>
    <span class="feature-label">All of them. Exception blocks, calli, varargs, generics, pinned locals, and tokens too.</span>
  </div>
  <div class="feature-item">
    <span class="feature-key">.class</span>
    <span class="feature-label">Types and methods that persist across cells. A struct shows its fields; a static keeps its value.</span>
  </div>
  <div class="feature-item">
    <span class="feature-key">.save</span>
    <span class="feature-label">Writes the types, the methods, and the cell to disk as an assembly. .il prints them back as ILAsm.</span>
  </div>
</div>
